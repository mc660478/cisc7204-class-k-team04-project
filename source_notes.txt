5. 2017 MODEL CHANGE AND RESIDUAL COMPARABILITY LIMIT
The original 2017 Q4 release reported an annual overall RPPI of 252.8.
The later 2019 historical table and current official database report 244.8.
This release consistently uses 244.8, not the superseded 252.8.
DSEC's Principal Indicators of Construction Sector 2018 Q4, Table 2.6, page 4,
explains that the pricing-model reference period changed to three consecutive months
from 2017 and indices from 2017 were revised accordingly. The official 2011 base
continues. Matching current values resolves the vintage issue; it does not prove
that the pre-2017 and later model specifications are identical. No undocumented
harmonization or backcast is attempted. The 2018–2025 subperiod avoids the transition
and also shows prices falling relative to earnings. The full series is retained with
this explicit historical comparability limit.

6. POPULATION, EARNINGS AND UNCERTAINTY
The 2025 Employment Survey, pages 32–34, defines employed residents as local people
aged 16 and above who work for pay or profit for at least one hour in the reference
week, including specified unpaid family workers and people with job attachment.
Non-resident workers are excluded from this resident series. Gross earnings cover
all jobs; employee remuneration includes bonuses and allowances, while employer and
self-employed earnings refer to business profits. This is not household income.
The household survey excludes collective quarters and people who work in Macao
while living outside its residential coverage. Medians can change with composition;
the data do not follow a fixed panel of individuals.
The annual survey reports precision for some overall-population statistics. Its
reported precision for the overall median (e.g. MOP 18,000 in 2025) must not be
transferred to the resident median (MOP 20,500). A matched set of resident-median
uncertainty estimates for all 13 years is not used here. No error band is invented.

7. INTERPRETATION AND REPRODUCIBILITY
RPPI measures price change, not the purchase price of a chosen dwelling. Both series
are nominal. The ratio of their rebased indices compares changes since a chosen year.
Household earnings, savings, deposits, mortgage rates, dwelling quality and income
distribution are outside this comparison. No complete affordability, causal or
forecasting claim is made. All earlier observed base years were checked; their
comparison periods differ, so the resulting magnitudes are period-dependent.
The notebook reproduces the analysis from saved separate inputs and source snapshots.
Exact environment metadata, source hashes and execution checks accompany the release.
